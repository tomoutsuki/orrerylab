# Orrery Lab — Design and Experience Principles

## 1. Central Experience Concept

The website is a digital orrery: a coherent system of music, artists, tools, notes, and ideas moving around one emotional centre.

The metaphor must influence structure, movement, and relationships without turning the website into a literal solar-system simulation.

The desired result is:

> A poetic machine created to organise emotion.

## 2. Experience Sequence

The visitor should:

1. enter a distinct atmosphere;
2. recognise that the world is centred on music;
3. understand how its branches relate;
4. encounter a featured work;
5. choose a clear path to explore or contact the studio.

The experience should follow the principle:

> Enter first. Understand gradually.

## 3. Emotional Qualities

The interface should feel:

- elegant;
- mysterious but legible;
- emotional but restrained;
- technological but warm;
- cosmic but not fantastical;
- experimental but coherent;
- cinematic but usable;
- contemporary but not trend-dependent.

## 4. Visual Language

### Structural elements

- orbital paths;
- circles and ellipses in perspective;
- mechanical joints;
- measured spacing;
- astronomical or technical diagrams;
- relationship maps;
- waveforms and harmonic structures;
- annotations and metadata.

### Emotional elements

- warm metallic light;
- atmospheric gradients;
- cinematic negative space;
- translucent surfaces;
- imperfect textures;
- slow changes in illumination;
- project-specific visual accents.

The identity should feel like a scientific instrument designed to measure emotion.

## 5. Colour Direction

### Deep background

Use warm charcoal or near-black tones rather than pure black.

### Brass light

Use muted brass or aged gold for:

- orbital lines;
- selected headings;
- active states;
- icons;
- subtle reflections.

The metallic colour should communicate craft and time, not luxury or status.

### Lunar neutral

Use warm pale neutrals for:

- primary text;
- editorial surfaces;
- Notes content;
- high-contrast areas.

### Controlled depth colour

Use restrained grey-blue or similar cool tones sparingly for depth, focus, and interaction.

### Project accents

Individual projects may introduce their own accent colours without changing the stable Orrery foundation.

## 6. Typography

### Display type

A refined serif for:

- major statements;
- project titles;
- manifesto fragments;
- artist identity moments.

### Interface type

A contemporary, multilingual sans serif for:

- navigation;
- body copy;
- controls;
- descriptions;
- forms.

### Technical type

A monospaced typeface used sparingly for:

- dates;
- coordinates;
- versions;
- technical labels;
- audio metadata.

The site must remain an art and music environment, not resemble a developer portfolio.

## 7. Motion Language

Motion communicates relationships rather than decoration.

### Orbital motion

Elements follow slow elliptical paths with subtle depth.

### Alignment

Bodies briefly align to indicate:

- navigation transitions;
- selection;
- loading completion;
- featured content.

### Resonance

Selecting one element should subtly influence nearby elements.

### Breathing

Very slow expansion and contraction may prevent the system from feeling sterile.

### Momentum

Scrolling influences a system already in motion rather than directly dragging every element.

### Restraint

Avoid:

- constant spinning;
- excessive particles;
- aggressive parallax;
- repeated loading theatre;
- motion without semantic purpose.

## 8. Sound Principles

Sound is optional, quiet, sparse, and user-controlled.

### Entry choices

- Enter with sound
- Enter in silence

### Possible interface sounds

- short harmonic emergence on entry;
- subtle tonal variation on branch focus;
- project-related transition sound;
- resolved tone when returning to the centre.

### Rules

- no loud autoplay;
- no full track before consent;
- sound toggle always visible;
- interface sounds must not repeat excessively;
- visual equivalents must exist for sonic information.

## 9. Interaction Principles

Every interaction should reveal meaning or improve navigation.

Appropriate interactions:

- cursor influence on perspective;
- subtle audio-reactive lines during playback;
- project relationship maps;
- gentle time-based atmosphere;
- generative arrangements that preserve predictable navigation.

Avoid interaction added only to demonstrate technical ability.

## 10. Responsive Translation

The mobile experience should translate the concept rather than reproduce desktop complexity.

Recommended mobile behaviour:

- central mark;
- vertical orbital arcs;
- large touch targets;
- subtle scroll motion;
- clear branch links;
- sound off by default;
- static or reduced-motion fallbacks.

## 11. Accessibility Principles

The expressive orbital interface is an enhancement. A complete structural website must exist beneath it.

Required support:

- semantic HTML;
- keyboard navigation;
- screen-reader labels;
- visible focus states;
- reduced-motion mode;
- sufficient contrast;
- text alternatives;
- transcripts and captions;
- mute controls;
- conventional menus;
- readable type sizes;
- touch-friendly controls.

## 12. Performance Principles

The site should feel rich without being wasteful.

- Prefer SVG and lightweight canvas for most orbital visuals.
- Use WebGL only when it provides unique value.
- Lazy-load media.
- Compress audio previews.
- Avoid large autoplay videos.
- Pause hidden animations.
- reduce activity when idle;
- support static fallbacks;
- respect battery-saving and reduced-motion preferences;
- load core content before artistic enhancements.

Efficiency should express the same precision as the visual system.

## 13. Avoided Directions

### Generic space branding

Avoid galaxies, astronauts, random stars, neon planets, and science-fiction dashboards.

### Generic electronic-music branding

Avoid constant glitch effects, equaliser visuals, distorted typography everywhere, and nightclub aesthetics.

### Generic luxury branding

Avoid excessive gold, fashion-editorial imitation, ornamental typography without substance, and empty minimalism.

### Generic technology branding

Avoid floating glass cards, corporate SaaS layouts, artificial-intelligence imagery, and unsupported innovation claims.

## 14. Success Criteria

The design succeeds when:

- the visitor can identify music as the centre;
- all branches feel related but distinct;
- primary navigation remains clear without motion or sound;
- the atmosphere feels original without relying on generic space imagery;
- animations support orientation and meaning;
- project content remains readable and accessible;
- the site performs well on mobile and modest hardware;
- the first encounter creates curiosity without delaying access.
