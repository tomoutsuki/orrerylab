# Orrery Lab — Website Information Architecture

## 1. Architecture Principle

The website should represent Orrery Lab as one connected creative system rather than a collection of independent categories.

The expressive interface may use orbital relationships, but the underlying architecture must remain conventional, semantic, and easy to navigate.

## 2. Launch Sitemap

```text
/
├── works/
│   └── [project-slug]/
├── artists/
│   ├── keyaki/
│   └── rei-tsukigaki/
├── about/
├── contact/
├── privacy/
└── accessibility/
```

The following sections should be added only when meaningful content exists:

```text
├── instruments/
│   └── [instrument-slug]/
└── notes/
    ├── [article-slug]/
    └── categories/[category-slug]/
```

## 3. Global Navigation

Persistent navigation should include:

- Orrery symbol / Home
- Works
- Artists
- Instruments, when active
- Notes, when active
- About
- Contact
- Sound toggle
- Language selector, when multilingual content is ready

A radial or orbital menu may enhance the experience, but a conventional navigation structure must remain available for keyboard, mobile, assistive technology, and reduced-motion use.

## 4. Homepage Structure

### 4.1 Entry layer

- Orrery symbol formation or static mark
- “There is still beauty in this world.”
- “Music for worlds between genres.”
- Enter in silence
- Enter with sound
- Skip intro

### 4.2 Brand centre

- concise studio definition;
- central orbital composition;
- links to active branches.

### 4.3 System overview

Short descriptions:

- Works — music created to remain after the moment has passed.
- Artists — distinct identities within one creative universe.
- Instruments — tools emerging from real artistic needs.
- Notes — ideas, processes, and discoveries behind the work.
- About — the purpose holding the system together.

### 4.4 Featured work

- artwork or visualiser;
- title;
- project type;
- emotional statement;
- minimal audio preview;
- project link.

### 4.5 Manifesto fragment

One short statement, not the complete manifesto.

### 4.6 Current activity

Up to three selected items:

- latest or featured composition;
- active experiment;
- recent note.

### 4.7 Collaboration invitation

- short invitation;
- link to contact or collaboration form.

## 5. Works Architecture

The Works section is the artistic centre of the site.

### Views

- **Featured view** — curated projects with expressive presentation.
- **Timeline view** — chronological history.
- **Archive view** — accessible list or grid.
- **Relationship view** — optional visual map based on shared themes, artists, or creative elements.

### Filters

Use only when the catalogue is large enough:

- release;
- commission;
- rhythm game;
- game or interactive media;
- soundtrack;
- collaboration;
- experiment.

### Project page structure

1. Hero visual or visualiser
2. Project title and role
3. Emotional statement
4. Audio or media player
5. Project overview
6. Creative orbit / relationship diagram
7. Process or selected development notes
8. Credits
9. Availability and external links
10. Related works

## 6. Artists Architecture

### Artists index

The index should show artist identities as different trajectories from the same Orrery centre.

### Artist page structure

1. Identity statement
2. Visual and sonic character
3. Selected works
4. Short biography
5. Creative focus
6. Credits and external profiles
7. Relationship to Orrery Lab

Do not present the artist identities as unrelated brands or direct competitors.

## 7. Instruments Architecture

### Instruments index

Organise by practical use rather than corporate product categories:

- composition;
- rhythm;
- MIDI;
- workflow;
- interaction;
- experiments.

### Instrument page structure

1. Name and status
2. Creative problem
3. What it enables
4. Demonstration or media
5. Use in Orrery projects
6. Intended audience
7. Availability, licence, or repository
8. Documentation
9. Related works and notes

## 8. Notes Architecture

### Categories

- Composition
- Sound
- Technology
- Process
- Culture
- Field Notes

### Notes index metadata

- title;
- publication date;
- category;
- related work;
- reading time;
- optional media indicator.

### Article structure

1. Title and introduction
2. Main content
3. Media, diagrams, or audio examples
4. References or footnotes
5. Related work
6. Related notes
7. Author and publication metadata

## 9. About Architecture

1. Essence
2. Orrery system diagram
3. Studio positioning
4. Creative method
5. Artist identities
6. Services and collaboration scope
7. Founder or practical identity
8. Short manifesto
9. Link to complete manifesto
10. Contact

The emotional purpose should lead. Professional information should follow and substantiate it.

## 10. Contact Architecture

### Opening

> Bring a world into Orrery.

### Form fields

- name;
- email or preferred contact;
- project type;
- short project description;
- approximate schedule;
- budget range, when appropriate;
- relevant links;
- consent acknowledgment.

### Project type options

- original music;
- rhythm game;
- game or interactive media;
- arrangement or orchestration;
- collaboration;
- creative technology;
- other.

### Confirmation

Use clear confirmation first, followed by restrained brand language:

> Your message has been received. Your signal has entered Orrery.

## 11. Utility Pages

Required utility pages should include:

- privacy policy;
- accessibility statement;
- legal or copyright notice;
- media credits, when needed;
- 404 page;
- error and offline states.

These pages should remain visually consistent but prioritise clarity over theatrical presentation.
