# Orrery Lab — Website Content Strategy

## 1. Content Objective

The website should help visitors understand Orrery Lab gradually rather than explaining the entire brand immediately.

The content journey should move through three stages:

1. **Feel the world** — encounter atmosphere, movement, sound, and a concise statement.
2. **Understand the system** — discover the relationship between works, artists, tools, notes, and philosophy.
3. **Take action** — listen, explore a project, contact the studio, or follow an artist.

## 2. Primary Communication Goals

The website must communicate, in order of importance:

1. Orrery Lab creates original, emotionally driven music.
2. Its work crosses genres and disciplines coherently.
3. Music is the centre; technology and writing extend the work.
4. Different artist identities belong to one creative system.
5. Orrery Lab is available for selected commissions and collaborations.

## 3. Messaging Hierarchy

### Primary brand line

> Music for worlds between genres.

### Supporting statement

> Orrery Lab is an independent music and creative-technology studio creating emotionally driven work for rhythm games, interactive media, and experimental projects.

### Philosophical statement

> We create to remind people that beauty can still be found in this world.

These statements should not always appear together. Use them according to context:

- the brand line introduces;
- the supporting statement clarifies;
- the philosophical statement deepens emotional understanding.

## 4. Content Principles

### Lead with meaning

Explain why a project exists before detailing its tools or production techniques.

### Remain specific

Replace broad claims such as “unique experiences” with concrete descriptions of emotional purpose, musical language, context, or function.

### Use restraint

Not every section should sound like a manifesto. Emotional language is most effective when surrounded by clear, practical writing.

### Reveal progressively

Use short homepage fragments and deeper project pages. Do not place the complete philosophy on the first screen.

### Connect every content type to real work

- Notes should emerge from genuine questions or completed projects.
- Instruments should solve real creative problems.
- Process content should support a work rather than become an independent content obligation.

### Preserve human authorship

Technology, code, and AI may be discussed, but always in relation to artistic intention and human responsibility.

## 5. Tone of Voice

The website should sound:

- thoughtful;
- elegant;
- emotionally aware;
- contemporary;
- confident;
- clear;
- subtly poetic;
- warmly intellectual.

It should avoid:

- exaggerated corporate language;
- empty innovation terminology;
- unnecessary technical jargon;
- artificial mystery;
- trend-driven slang;
- constant self-promotion;
- describing every work as revolutionary or groundbreaking.

## 6. Writing Patterns

### Project description formula

Use the following structure:

1. **What it is** — project type and context.
2. **Emotional centre** — what the work seeks to make the audience feel.
3. **Creative relationship** — the worlds, genres, or ideas brought together.
4. **Role and outcome** — Orrery Lab’s contribution and where the work can be experienced.

Example:

> A grand-finals composition created for a rhythm-game tournament. Built around the feeling of crossing an immense and unfamiliar landscape, the piece brings orchestral narrative, electronic intensity, and Brazilian cultural references into one continuous musical arc.

### Tool description formula

1. The creative problem.
2. What the instrument allows.
3. Where it was used.
4. Current availability or maturity.

### Note introduction formula

1. The question or observation.
2. Why it matters to the work.
3. What the reader will learn or explore.

## 7. Homepage Content Priorities

The homepage should contain only enough language to orient and invite exploration.

Recommended content sequence:

1. Brand line.
2. One-sentence studio definition.
3. Brief explanation of the Orrery system.
4. Short branch descriptions.
5. One featured work.
6. One manifesto fragment.
7. Current activity or selected updates.
8. Collaboration invitation.

Avoid:

- long founder biographies;
- complete service descriptions;
- dense technical explanations;
- an extensive news feed;
- large grids of equal-priority projects;
- empty future sections.

## 8. Calls to Action

Primary calls to action:

- Explore the works
- Listen to the project
- Meet the artists
- Read the process
- Collaborate with Orrery
- Enter the archive

Secondary calls to action:

- View credits
- Open the instrument
- Read the note
- Return to the centre
- Listen on external platforms

Calls to action should be direct and understandable. Orbital metaphors may support them, but must not replace clarity.

## 9. Content Pillars

### Works

Finished music, commissions, collaborations, releases, and interactive projects.

### Artists

The individual creative identities operating within Orrery Lab.

### Instruments

Tools and systems emerging from artistic practice.

### Notes

Process, research, observations, and cultural or technical exploration.

### About

The purpose, system, people, services, and manifesto.

## 10. Content Governance

Each published item should have:

- a clear owner;
- a defined status;
- a publication date;
- an update date when materially revised;
- related works or artist identities;
- accessible media alternatives;
- complete credits;
- verified external links.

Do not publish placeholder sections only to imply scale. A smaller, complete website is preferable to a larger, empty system.
